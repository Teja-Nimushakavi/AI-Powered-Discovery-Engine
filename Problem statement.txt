AI-Powered Wishlist Purchase Discovery Engine
Background
Myntra has millions of users who browse fashion products and add products to their wishlist. Adding a product to a wishlist represents an explicit expression of interest, but many wishlisted products do not convert into purchases within 30 days.
The business goal is to increase the percentage of users who purchase at least one item from their wishlist within 30 days of adding it.
However, the underlying reasons for this conversion gap are unknown.
The Product Manager should not assume that the problem is price, size, fit, reviews, styling, or any other predefined factor.
Problem Statement
Myntra lacks a scalable, evidence-based way to discover and prioritize the underlying customer problems that prevent users from converting wishlisted fashion products into purchases within 30 days.
User conversations about fashion shopping are distributed across app-store reviews, Reddit, YouTube, social media, product reviews, Q&A discussions, and other public sources. Manually analyzing these conversations is time-consuming and makes it difficult to identify recurring behavioral patterns, distinguish high-impact problems from frequently mentioned but low-impact issues, understand differences across user segments, and connect discovered problems to the wishlist-to-purchase conversion goal.
Therefore, we need to build an AI-powered Discovery Engine that analyzes large-scale public user feedback and conversations to discover, quantify, validate, and prioritize the problems and unmet needs that may prevent wishlisted products from progressing to purchase.
Core Discovery Question
Why do users add fashion products to their wishlist but fail to purchase at least one of those products within 30 days?
The AI system must discover the answer from evidence rather than starting with a predetermined answer.
What the AI Agent Must Discover
The agent should investigate:
1. Wishlist Motivation
Why do users add products to their wishlist?
For example:
* Genuine purchase intent
* Bookmarking
* Comparison
* Future purchase
* Waiting for the right occasion
* Waiting for more information
* Saving multiple alternatives
These are hypotheses, not predetermined conclusions.
2. Purchase Barriers
What prevents users from progressing from wishlist to purchase?
Potential barriers may include:
* Fit or size uncertainty
* Quality uncertainty
* Product appearance uncertainty
* Lack of trustworthy reviews
* Conflicting reviews
* Price hesitation
* Comparison with alternatives
* Styling uncertainty
* Occasion uncertainty
* Social validation
* Return/exchange concerns
* Delivery concerns
* Product availability
* Decision overload
* Loss of purchase urgency
The agent must also discover barriers that are not included in this list.
3. Information Gaps
What information do users still need after they have already identified a product they like?
Identify whether users seek information about:
* Fit
* Size
* Fabric/material
* Quality
* Appearance
* Styling
* Reviews
* Real-user photos
* Alternatives
* Occasion suitability
* Return policy
* Availability
* Other factors
4. Decision-Making Behavior
Understand how users make the final decision between:
Browse → Like → Wishlist → Evaluate → Compare → Decide → Purchase
Identify where users get stuck and why.
5. External Research
Identify what information users seek outside Myntra before purchasing.
Examples:
* Google searches
* Reddit discussions
* YouTube reviews
* Instagram/social content
* Other fashion platforms
* Other product reviews
6. User Segmentation
Identify meaningful differences in wishlist behavior across user segments.
For example:
* High-intent shoppers
* Exploratory shoppers
* Comparison shoppers
* Occasion-driven shoppers
* Fit-conscious shoppers
* Price-sensitive shoppers
* Social-validation seekers
The AI should discover or validate these segments rather than assuming they exist.

AI Discovery Workflow
The system should follow this pipeline:
Collect → Clean → Classify → Extract → Cluster → Quantify → Segment → Prioritize → Generate Insights
Step 1 — Collect
Ingest publicly available fashion-shopping conversations from multiple sources.
Initial MVP sources may include:
* Google Play reviews
* Apple App Store reviews
* Reddit
* YouTube comments
* Public fashion communities
* Product reviews
* Product Q&A
* Other publicly available shopping discussions
The MVP should not attempt to integrate every source initially.
Start with a manageable dataset and design the architecture so additional sources can be added later.
Step 2 — Clean
The AI should identify and remove:
* Duplicates
* Spam
* Advertisements
* Irrelevant content
* Empty content
* Non-shopping conversations
Original evidence should be preserved.
Step 3 — Classify Relevance
Classify each record as:
* Relevant to wishlist/purchase behavior
* Relevant to fashion purchase decision
* Potentially relevant
* Irrelevant
The system should explain why a record was considered relevant when necessary.
Step 4 — Extract Behavioral Signals
For relevant conversations, identify signals such as:
* Product interest
* Wishlist/save behavior
* Purchase intent
* Purchase hesitation
* Delayed purchase
* Comparison
* Information seeking
* Abandoned purchase
* Post-purchase feedback
Step 5 — Discover Problems
Use semantic AI analysis to identify recurring problems and themes.
Do not force every conversation into predefined categories.
The system should allow new themes to emerge from the data.
Each problem should contain:
* Problem name
* Problem description
* User need
* Trigger
* Barrier
* Affected segment
* Evidence
* Confidence level
AI-based thematic analysis should group feedback by meaning rather than simply matching keywords, while retaining transparency around how themes were formed.
Step 6 — Quantify
For every discovered problem calculate, where data permits:
* Number of mentions
* Percentage of relevant feedback
* Number of unique users/sources
* Number of platforms
* Product categories affected
* Trend over time
* Severity
* Segment distribution
Never fabricate statistics.
If evidence is insufficient, explicitly state:
"Insufficient evidence."
Step 7 — Identify Root Causes
Do not stop at:
"Users complain about size."
Go deeper.
Example:
Surface signal:
Users mention size frequently.
↓
Underlying problem:
Users are uncertain whether the displayed size information will accurately predict real-world fit.
↓
Potential unmet need:
Users need greater confidence about product fit before committing to purchase.
The agent should distinguish between:
Observation → Interpretation → Hypothesis
so AI-generated assumptions are not presented as facts.
Step 8 — Segment
For each important problem identify which users are affected.
Example:
Segment
	Problem
	Evidence
	Potential impact
	First-time fashion shoppers
	Fit uncertainty
	High
	High
	Frequent shoppers
	Comparison overload
	Medium
	Medium
	Occasion shoppers
	Styling uncertainty
	Medium
	High
	These are examples only. Actual segments must come from the evidence.
Step 9 — Prioritize Opportunities
Do not rank opportunities only by frequency.
A high-frequency problem is not automatically the most important problem.
Score opportunities using:
Opportunity Priority = Frequency × Impact × Conversion Relevance × Evidence Confidence
Where:
* Frequency = how commonly the problem appears
* Impact = potential severity of the barrier
* Conversion Relevance = how strongly the problem could prevent wishlist-to-purchase conversion
* Evidence Confidence = strength and consistency of supporting evidence
Also consider:
* User segment importance
* Trend
* Strategic relevance
* Potential effort to address
This prevents the system from simply selecting the loudest complaint and instead focuses on problems that could matter to the business goal.
Step 10 — Generate Opportunity Areas
The final output should identify the highest-potential customer problems.
For each opportunity provide:
Opportunity
Users struggle with ______.
Affected segment
Primarily ______ users.
Evidence
Supported by ______ pieces of feedback across ______ sources.
Frequency
______% of relevant feedback.
Potential impact
High / Medium / Low.
Why it matters
This may prevent users from progressing from wishlist to purchase because ______.
Confidence
High / Medium / Low.
What needs validation
______.
The system should recommend what to investigate next, rather than immediately jumping to a product feature.

The AI agent should generate a Product Manager-ready report containing:
1. Executive Summary
Top discovered opportunities.
2. Wishlist Behavior
Why users wishlist products.
3. Purchase Barriers
Why wishlisted products do not convert.
4. Uncertainty Map
What users remain uncertain about before purchasing.
5. User Segments
Different wishlist behaviors and their barriers.

Top opportunities ranked by evidence, impact and conversion relevance.

Source-linked evidence supporting each major insight.
8. Unexpected Findings
Important problems that were not part of the initial hypotheses.
9. Knowledge Gaps
What the available public data cannot answer.
10. Recommended Next Research

* Surveys

Important Constraints
The AI agent must:
1. Not assume the customer problem beforehand.
2. Not jump directly to a solution.
3. Not rely only on sentiment analysis.
4. Not rely only on keyword matching.
5. Not prioritize problems purely by frequency.
6. Not invent statistics or evidence.
7. Maintain traceability from insight to source evidence.
8. Separate facts, observations, hypotheses and conclusions.
9. Identify both common and unexpected problems.
10. Connect discovered opportunities to the 30-day wishlist-to-purchase goal.
11. Not recommend monetary incentives such as discounts, coupons, cashback or price reductions as the solution.
12. Clearly identify limitations caused by public-feedback data and the silent majority who may not post feedback.
Public feedback alone cannot fully represent all users because many users never leave feedback, so the agent should explicitly identify this limitation and recommend validating important findings against behavioral/product analytics.
MVP
The first version should be simple.
Input
Upload a CSV containing fashion-shopping reviews/comments.
AI Agent
Analyze the dataset automatically.
Output
Display:
* Total feedback analyzed
* Relevant feedback
* Top problems
* Problem frequency
* Problem severity
* User segments
* Evidence
* Opportunity score
* Top 3–5 opportunities
* Knowledge gaps
* Recommended next research
Future Version
Add automated data collection from multiple public sources.
The architecture should therefore separate:
Data Collection → AI Analysis → Opportunity Intelligence → Dashboard
so new data sources can be added without rebuilding the entire system.
Success Criteria for the Agent
The agent is successful if a Product Manager can go from:
Thousands of unstructured user conversations
to:
A small number of evidence-backed customer problems
and finally to:
Prioritized opportunity areas that can be investigated for their potential to improve wishlist-to-purchase conversion.
The agent should function as an AI Product Discovery Analyst, not merely as a review summarizer or sentiment-analysis chatbot.
